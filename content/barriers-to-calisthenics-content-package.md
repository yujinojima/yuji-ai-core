## 2. LinkedIn Post

I spent years studying why people fail to change their behaviour.

Not in a gym. In a lab. In classrooms. In clinical settings. My Master's is in Applied Behaviour Analysis — the science of why humans do what they do (and don't do what they say they want to).

Then I turned that lens on fitness.

And what I found was this: the biggest barrier to starting calisthenics isn't strength. It's not flexibility. It's not time.

It's the absence of a clear first step.

Think about it. When someone joins a gym, they get an induction. A trainer walks them through machines. There's a path.

Calisthenics doesn't have that. You Google "beginner calisthenics" and get a video of someone doing a planche. You scroll Instagram and see human flags. You think: "That's not for me."

In behavioural science, we call this the "response effort" problem. When the perceived effort to even figure out what to do is too high, the behaviour never starts. It's not laziness. It's a design flaw in how the information is presented.

Here's what else I see:

- People follow rules nobody set ("I need a pull-up bar to start")
- People compare their Day 0 to someone else's Year 5
- People wait until they're "fit enough" — which is like waiting until you can swim before getting in the pool

These are behavioural barriers, not physical ones. And they have behavioural solutions.

That's why I built my coaching around removing friction before adding exercises. We don't start with a workout plan. We start with: what's actually stopping you?
