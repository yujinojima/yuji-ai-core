# Calibration Example: GAMSAT Section 2 Passage

## Passage map

- **L1:** GAMSAT Section 2 writing is not normal essay writing.
- **L2:** The origin of a 90+ response is what the writer makes from the task, especially how the quotes are interpreted.
- **L3:** Quality, sophisticated ideas make a poor response difficult, whereas simple or reductive ideas make a strong response difficult.
- **L4:** Quote interpretation functions as the ceiling value of the writing.
- **L5:** It sets the upper limit; delivery determines how much of that potential is realised.
- **L6:** Many students create a “glass ceiling” by interpreting the task in a rushed and pedestrian way.
- **L7:** This weak beginning makes a high-scoring response unlikely.

## Epistemic anchors

### ER-01 — Task representation as interpretation

- **Lines:** L1–L2
- **Resource content:** Represent Section 2 primarily as a meaning-construction task, not as reproduction of a normal essay template.
- **Function:** Task framing.
- **Textual status:** EXPLICIT
- **Why it matters:** The representation chosen at the start determines which strategies become relevant.
- **Competing naïve resource:** “A polished standard essay structure is the main source of marks.”
- **Activation cue:** Before planning, ask: “What relationship among the quotes can I construct?”

### ER-02 — Idea-quality constraint

- **Lines:** L3
- **Resource content:** Evaluate the conceptual quality of an interpretation separately from its verbal delivery.
- **Function:** Quality criterion and causal inference.
- **Textual status:** EXPLICIT
- **Why it matters:** It prevents fluency, vocabulary, or structure from disguising a shallow proposition.
- **Competing naïve resource:** “Sophisticated language can compensate for a simple idea.”
- **Activation cue:** State the core idea in one plain sentence and test whether it remains non-reductive.

### ER-03 — Ceiling-versus-realisation model

- **Lines:** L4–L5
- **Resource content:** Model interpretation as potential and delivery as the proportion of that potential realised.
- **Function:** Relational analogy.
- **Textual status:** EXPLICIT
- **Why it matters:** It separates two linked but non-identical determinants of performance.
- **Competing naïve resource:** “Interpretation and writing quality are one undifferentiated skill.”
- **Activation cue:** Ask separately: “How high is the idea ceiling?” and “How effectively am I realising it?”

### ER-04 — Metacognitive anti-rushing rule

- **Lines:** L6–L7
- **Resource content:** Monitor the speed and depth of quote interpretation because premature closure constrains the entire response.
- **Function:** Regulation.
- **Textual status:** INFERRED
- **Why it matters:** The author criticises rushed interpretation; the regulatory rule is strongly implied rather than directly instructed.
- **Competing naïve resource:** “Choose the first coherent theme quickly so there is more time to write.”
- **Activation cue:** Generate at least two competing interpretations before committing.

## ABCDE reasoning units

### Unit 1 — Interpretation is the primary origin of response quality

| Move | Reconstruction | Status | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | High-level Section 2 performance originates primarily in how the task and quotes are interpreted. | EXPLICIT | L1–L2 |
| B | Strong ideas rarely produce wholly poor responses, while simple or reductive ideas rarely produce strong ones. | EXPLICIT | L3 |
| C | The quality of the underlying proposition constrains the quality of any essay built from it. | INFERRED | L2–L3 |
| C1 | No external evidence or scoring data are supplied in the excerpt. | ABSENT | — |
| C2 | The claims are framed strongly, but “very hard” implies tendency rather than impossibility. | EXPLICIT | L3 |
| D | Strong delivery might partly rescue a modest interpretation, and poor delivery may still damage a sophisticated one. | INFERRED | L3, L5 |
| E | In any reasoning task, upstream problem representation may constrain the value of downstream execution. | TUTOR EXTENSION | — |

- **Plain-language logic:** The contrast in L3 supports the central claim because the initial idea limits what later writing can express.
- **Resource interaction:** Task representation + idea-quality discrimination + warrant recovery.
- **What the passage leaves open:** It does not operationally define “quality” or show how examiners distinguish sophisticated from merely complicated ideas.

- **Plain-language logic:** The reported pattern supports the claim because early shallow representation constrains the response's later possibilities.
- **Resource interaction:** Metacognitive monitoring + anti-premature-closure rule + quality discrimination.
- **What the passage leaves open:** The specific procedure that reliably transforms extra interpretation time into stronger ideas.

## Architecture

- **Central claim:** The conceptual interpretation of the quotes is the upstream determinant of Section 2 potential.
- **Deepest warrant:** Downstream expression can realise or lose conceptual value, but cannot reliably substitute for an absent underlying idea.
- **Highest-leverage resource:** Separating idea quality from delivery quality.
- **Most important missing move:** A backed and operational account of what makes an interpretation sophisticated rather than reductive.
- **Performance distinction:** Interpretation establishes conceptual potential; writing craft controls realisation.
