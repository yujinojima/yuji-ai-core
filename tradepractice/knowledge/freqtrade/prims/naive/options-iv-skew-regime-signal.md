### Mechanism

The Deribit BTC/ETH options market prices the forward distribution of returns into the implied volatility (IV) surface. The 25-delta put-call skew captures the **distributional asymmetry** priced by options participants: how much more IV is assigned to the 25th-percentile downside move than to the 25th-percentile upside move.

When skew > +5%, options market participants are paying a material premium for downside protection. Two mechanistic channels create predictive content:

**Channel 1 — Information lead (Pan & Poteshman 2006):**
Options buyers (particularly put buyers) are systematically better-informed than spot/perp traders. Institutional hedgers, macro funds, and market makers who have existing long exposure buy puts when they believe downside risk has increased. They have longer time horizons and access to fundamental analysis unavailable to retail perpetual traders. Their put demand lifts put IV relative to call IV, creating positive skew as a leading signal before the directional move is observable in the spot/perp market.

**Channel 2 — Delta-hedging mechanical flow (Bates 2000, Gârleanu et al. 2009):**
Market makers who sell puts (to the institutional hedgers described above) must delta-hedge. Selling puts creates negative gamma exposure — as price falls, the market maker's delta becomes more negative, requiring them to sell spot/perp to rebalance. This creates a self-reinforcing mechanical selling flow that amplifies downside moves once initiated. Elevated put IV is therefore both a *leading indicator* (information channel) and a *causal amplifier* (flow channel) of subsequent price declines.

**Why 25-delta specifically:**
- 25-delta strikes represent the "near tail" — approximately 1-standard-deviation moves over the expiry horizon
- They are liquid enough to have reliable IV marks (unlike far OTM options, where thin liquidity produces unreliable IV)
- They capture institutional hedging demand specifically: speculative directional options buyers prefer ATM; tail-risk hedgers prefer 25-delta
- The Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou (2009) variance risk premium result is concentrated in this near-tail region

### Conditions

**Works when:**
- Institutional hedgers are active: large BTC/ETH fund exposure drives put demand
- Pre-drawdown accumulation of hedges: options market leads spot by 1–3 days (Pan & Poteshman 2006 equity analogue; crypto lead time unknown)
- IV skew is elevated vs historical baseline (not during structural elevated periods where skew is baseline noise)
- Deribit remains the dominant BTC/ETH options venue (> 70% market share)

**Fails when:**
- Crypto options market is structurally different from equity options: American-exercise differences, perpetual futures substitution for spot, yield curve absence (no interest rate term structure)
- Skew elevated by supply-side dynamics: covered call sellers on exchanges structurally suppress call IV, creating mechanical skew even without downside information
- CME BTC options take dominance post-ETF: CME skew dynamics may differ from Deribit (institutional vs retail participant split inverted)
- Very low options volume: thin liquidity creates unreliable IV marks, especially for 25-delta options
- Expiry mechanics near options settlement: gamma effects in final 24h before expiry create noise unrelated to directional information

### 10 Documented Limitations (Blocking Prerequisites for Intermediate)

1. **Threshold uncalibrated from crypto data** — The +5%/+10%/−2% thresholds are S&P 500 analogues from Bates (2000) adjusted for crypto volatility; actual crypto BTC/ETH skew distribution requires calibration from Deribit historical data (2020–2025); frequency of skew > +5% events unknown; threshold may be far too low (crypto skew is structurally higher than equity) or appropriate
2. **Lead time vs spot market unknown** — Pan & Poteshman (2006) documents 1–2 day lead in equity options; crypto options-to-spot lead time is uncharacterized; if lead < 1 bar (1h), suppression would fire too late; if lead > 5 days, the 4h refresh provides too-frequent stale signal; empirical characterization required before intermediate elevation
3. **No regime gate** — elevated skew during genuine bear trends should AMPLIFY downside (trend aligned), not merely suppress longs; suppression is only appropriate in ranging-to-mildly-bearish regimes; the naive rule treats all elevated skew as suppression regardless of trend context
4. **Structural skew vs informational skew unresolved** — BTC options have a structural skew premium in all regimes (risk premium for tail risk); the 5% threshold may be entirely in the structural baseline with zero informational content; requires decomposition into structural and informational components (implied by term structure comparison: near-term skew vs 1-month skew deviation)
5. **Data complexity vs existing prims** — premiumIndex (basis prim) requires one REST call; fundingRate (funding prim) requires one REST call; IV skew requires: (a) listing all active options, (b) identifying target expiry, (c) finding 25-delta strikes, (d) extracting IV marks — 4x more complex; backtest without live data requires Deribit historical tick data subscription (not public like Binance klines)
6. **CME vs Deribit venue split** — post-January 2024 ETF approval: CME BTC options open interest grew significantly; if CME skew diverges from Deribit skew, which leads? Deribit remains dominant by OI but CME has distinct institutional participant base; venue risk uncharacterized
7. **Expiry selection sensitivity** — skew is expiry-specific; weekly vs monthly vs quarterly expiries have different skew levels; which expiry to use is a parameter that must be calibrated (nearest expiry has highest gamma noise; far expiry is more signal-rich but less timely)
8. **Correlation with existing derivatives prims unknown** — IV skew may be correlated with funding rate (both measure institutional risk appetite) and OI divergence (OI falling + skew rising = reinforcing); N_eff impact on position sizing unquantified; if ρ(IV skew, funding) > 0.70, this axis adds little incremental information to the derivatives prim family
9. **Crypto-specific skew dynamics** — BTC/ETH options market is not a pure hedging market: a significant portion of options volume is speculative (leveraged call buying during bull markets, put buying during bear markets); the Pan & Poteshman (2006) "informed hedger" story may not transfer cleanly to a market where retail speculation dominates options flow in certain regimes
10. **No historical validation** — zero backtesting of IV skew as a meta-signal modifier on any of the 14 existing freqtrade prims; the entire mechanism is hypothesis-level; the naive prim documents a plausible mechanism with academic support but no own-data evidence; intermediate elevation requires at minimum a frequency scan and directional accuracy measurement
